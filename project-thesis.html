LLM features you can trust in a pricing model

Every pricing team adding LLM features will ask whether they can be trusted. This project builds the evaluation harness that answers it, then tests the features on a real price change: New York’s January 2025 congestion toll, 261 zones.

The LLM is treated as a measurement instrument, so it gets the same checks as a survey: reliability before use, and an ablation that asks whether its features improve the model at all.

Geo-experiment: what a real price change did to demand

Event study of ride-hail (HVFHV) share of road trips in the toll zone, relative to the month before the toll. After January 2025 the share ramps down to about −4 pp and stays negative.

Caveat, reported rather than hidden: the first two months of the panel sit above zero (data onboarding tail). Dropping them moves the estimate from −2.6 to −2.1 pp; the sign and significance hold. A formal sensitivity bound is the next robustness step.

LLM eval harness: where the noise comes from

For each trait, the share of score variance that is real zone-to-zone signal versus noise from the prompt wording, the choice of model, or random draws. Model disagreement, not wording, is the main noise source.

LLM eval harness: 14 of 15 features pass

Generalizability coefficient G per trait at the pilot design (5 wordings × 3 models × 3 draws). Pre-registered target: 0.85.

Perceived safety peaks at 0.835 and is flagged rather than dropped. Averaging across models is what buys reliability: a single model reaches 0.85 on only 3 of 15 traits.

What a pricing team would do with this

  • priceRead price response zone by zone instead of city-wide, so tolls, surcharges or discounts can differ by area.
  • evalsTest LLM-generated features for reliability before they reach a production model, not after a bad decision.
  • reuseRun the same harness on any entities and traits: stores, routes, merchants, with a frozen, versioned prompt spec.
